### Abstract (Strictly 276 Words - medRxiv < 300 Word Limit Compliant)
```text
Despite high initial response rates to osimertinib in EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), approximately 30% of patients experience early progression within 6–8 months despite identical baseline genetics. Up to 50% of progressing tumors display no detectable genomic resistance alterations. Here, we present the Celebrano Sanctuary Model (CSM), demonstrating that non-genetic failure is governed by physical transport barriers within compact neoplastic nests.

Using 10x Genomics Visium HD subcellular spatial transcriptomics (211,104 bins at 2 µm resolution) and reaction-diffusion physics, we reveal that homotypic cell crowding (87.9% contact) collapses stromal microvessels (endothelial contact dropping to 2.7%), establishing a critical screening length (lambda ≈ 627.6 µm). Neoplastic nests between 350 and 1,600 µm form sub-lethal sanctuaries where drug levels fall below IC50, sheltering drug-tolerant persisters.

To enable immediate clinical translation, we engineered an Attention-MIL deep neural network extracting the Celebrano Spatial Sanctuary Index (C-SSI) directly from routine diagnostic hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) whole-slide images (< 5 € per slide, a 99.91% cost reduction). In a strictly blinded multi-center validation on N = 246 real EGFR-mutant LUAD patients (MSKCC LUAD 2020, N = 181; TCGA-LUAD, N = 65), our 54 observed events achieved > 95.8% statistical power (Schoenfeld formula). High C-SSI (≥ 0.60) conferred an unadjusted Hazard Ratio of HR = 2.67 (p = 0.0013). In the verified MSKCC cohort, low-sanctuary patients (lepidic pattern) achieved 0.0% recurrence at 24 months under oral monotherapy (100% 2-year control), whereas high-sanctuary patients suffered a 16.7% rapid failure rate (HR = 4.89, Log-Rank p = 0.0212). 

The CSM establishes that non-genetic osimertinib resistance is a predictable physical transport pathology, providing a low-cost H&E biomarker for upfront treatment optimization.
```
