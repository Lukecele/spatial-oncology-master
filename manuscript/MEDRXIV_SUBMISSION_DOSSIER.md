4. ABSTRACT (CLEAN ASCII - NO SPECIAL CHARACTERS - 266 WORDS)
================================================================================
Despite high initial response rates to osimertinib in EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), therapeutic durability remains heterogeneous. Up to 50% of progressing tumors display no detectable genomic resistance alterations. Here, we present the Spatial Sanctuary Framework (SSF), resolving non-genetic persistence by linking theoretical evolutionary dynamics, subcellular spatial transcriptomics, reaction-diffusion physics, and clinical histopathology via the Spatial Sanctuary Index (SSI).

Using 10x Genomics Visium HD subcellular transcriptomics (211,104 bins at 2 um resolution), we demonstrate that homotypic tumor clustering in solid patterns (87.9% contact) collapses stromal microvessels (endothelial contact 2.7%). Modeling transport via cylindrical reaction-diffusion physics (D del^2 C - k C = 0), we find that while the intrinsic penetration depth spans lambda ~ 627 um, therapeutic levels fall below IC50 (12.4 nM) within 70-100 um from the nearest capillary. Because cells survive within the tissue oxygen diffusion limit (150-200 um), solid nests exceeding 200 um form viable pharmacological sanctuaries sheltering drug-tolerant cells.

Evaluating the SSI in a blinded real-world cohort of EGFR-mutant LUAD patients from Memorial Sloan Kettering (MSKCC LUAD 2020, N = 182), histopathologically verified high-sanctuary solid/micropapillary architectures experienced rapid disease recurrence compared to durable lepidic patterns (HR = 4.89, 95% CI: 1.78-13.46, Log-Rank p = 0.0212; 2-year recurrence-free rate 0.0% in lepidic vs 16.7% in solid). Across N = 247 combined patients with 54 events, the framework achieved > 95.8% statistical power (Schoenfeld analysis). Finally, InSituVision Attention-MIL extracts sanctuary risk directly from routine diagnostic H&E whole-slide images (< 5 EUR per slide, C-index ~ 0.74).

The SSF establishes that non-genetic resistance is a predictable physical transport pathology, providing an actionable foundation for precision oncology.
